# 02 — Personas & Empathy Maps

> **⚠ Evidence status.** Quotes carried through from
> [`01-research-data.md`](01-research-data.md) are `⟨proxy⟩` until you have run
> the sessions. The persona *structure* is final; only the evidence needs
> replacing.

Three personas, one per stakeholder category identified in
[§3 of the research](01-research-data.md#3-stakeholder-categories). Each is a
composite of the participants listed in its evidence row — not a single person
renamed, and not an invention. Where a persona attribute has no participant
behind it, it is not in the persona.

Each persona is followed by an **empathy map** in the standard four quadrants
(Says / Thinks / Does / Feels) plus Pains and Gains. Every cell carries the
evidence ID it came from. A cell with no ID would be an assumption, and there
are none.

## Persona 1 — Kavitha Sivanesan — *The Occasional Renter*

**Category:** SC-1, Renter / Demand side
**Composite of:** P1, P2 · corroborated by SUR-Q1, SUR-Q5, SUR-Q6

|  |  |
|---|---|
| **Age** | 24 |
| **Location** | Nallur, Jaffna |
| **Occupation** | Trainee accountant |
| **Tech** | Android mid-range phone, mobile data, buys online occasionally, no card saved anywhere |
| **Sport** | Surfs 6–8 times a year at Casuarina; would go more often if getting a board were simpler |
| **Owns** | Nothing. A wetsuit top she was given |
| **Budget** | LKR 2,500–3,500 a day feels normal; LKR 5,000 held on a card feels like a lot |

### Her situation

Kavitha is not a surfer, she is someone who surfs. That distinction is the whole
persona. She does not know what volume board she should be on, she has never
owned one, and the last time she borrowed one it was too small and she came off
it onto reef — an event she still describes in terms of the scar rather than the
session (**P1-Q3**).

She finds equipment through a WhatsApp group with forty people in it, where a
question posted on Thursday gets answered on Monday (**P1-Q2**). She has driven
to a place that confirmed a booking on the phone and given it away by the time
she arrived (**P2-Q2**). Her problem is not price and it is not choice. It is
that nothing is *certain* until she is standing in front of it.

She will not complete a booking she does not understand. When the deposit screen
showed LKR 15,000 she stopped, and the sentence that unstuck her was not about
the amount (**P1-Q6**).

### Goals

| | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Get a board that is genuinely right for her body, without having to already know the answer | P1-Q3, SUR-Q5 |
| Know before she leaves the house that the equipment will actually be there | P1-Q2, P2-Q2 |
| Understand exactly what money leaves her account and what merely sits still | P1-Q6, SUR-Q6 |
| Collect it without a long negotiation with a stranger about times and places | P2-Q2, P1-Q2 |

### Frustrations

| | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Sizing advice assumes she already knows the vocabulary | P1-Q3 |
| Availability is a promise, not a fact | P1-Q2, P2-Q2 |
| A four-figure deposit reads as a charge, and she doesn't have it spare | P1-Q6, OBS-P1-T5 |
| One unexplained line on a bill makes her suspect the whole bill | OBS-P1-T7 |
| Being asked to photograph an item seven times in front of its owner feels like an accusation | P1-Q8, OBS-P1-T8 |

### Empathy map

| Quadrant | Content |
|---|---|
| **SAYS** | *"There's a group with maybe forty people in it. You post 'anyone got a 6'6 spare Saturday' and you get three replies on Monday."* (**P1-Q2**) · *"Fifteen thousand? For a weekend? No."* (**P1-Q6**) · *"What's this one? Is that yours? What's it for?"* (**OBS-P1-T7**) |
| **THINKS** | *Am I going to look stupid asking what size I need?* (**P1-Q3**) · *If it says fifteen thousand, that's fifteen thousand gone.* (**OBS-P1-T5**) · *Seven photographs is going to look like I don't trust him.* (**P1-Q8**) |
| **DOES** | Posts in a group chat and waits · Rings ahead and drives anyway (**P2-Q2**) · Reads a bill line by line before paying, and stops at the one she can't name (**OBS-P1-T7**) · Borrows whatever is available rather than what fits (**P1-Q3**) |
| **FEELS** | **Uncertain** up to the moment she is holding the equipment (**P1-Q2**) · **Exposed** by sizing questions she can't answer (**P1-Q3**) · **Alarmed** at the deposit, then **relieved** when it is explained (**P1-Q6**) · **Self-conscious** during the handover with someone she has never met (**P1-Q8**) |
| **PAINS** | Availability is unverifiable · Sizing knowledge she doesn't have · A deposit she reads as a debit · An unexplained fee · Social awkwardness of the handover ritual |
| **GAINS** | A verdict on fit she can act on without expertise · A calendar she can trust · The word *held*, not *charged*, at the size of the number · A collection point instead of a rendezvous |

### What she needs from the design

Fit stated as a verdict with a reason (**S-02**, **S-07**), availability that is
authoritative rather than aspirational (**S-08**), the deposit's *verb* made as
prominent as its amount (**S-09**), every bill line self-describing (**S-11**),
and a way to collect that is not a meeting (**S-13** GearPoint).

## Persona 2 — Roshan de Silva — *The Reluctant Lender*

**Category:** SC-2, Owner / Supply side
**Composite of:** P3, P4 · with P5 informing the swap-side attributes ·
corroborated by SUR-Q2

|  |  |
|---|---|
| **Age** | 34 |
| **Location** | Gurunagar, Jaffna |
| **Occupation** | Bank officer |
| **Tech** | Comfortable; uses online banking, wary of anything asking for a card |
| **Sport** | Surfs and paddles; two boards and a SUP, out maybe twice between May and September |
| **Owns** | ~LKR 180,000 of equipment. He knows the figure |
| **Motivation** | Not primarily money. Space, and the mild guilt of an expensive thing not being used |

### His situation

Roshan has a SUP behind a wardrobe that he is annoyed by every time he has to
move it (**P3-Q1**). He has thought about selling it and hasn't, because selling
is final and he might want it in July.

He would consider lending it. He will not lend it on a star rating. Asked what
would have to be true first, he did not name one condition, he named a stack:
identity held by the platform, money at stake, and photographs taken by both
people at the start so that nobody can invent a scratch afterwards
(**P3-Q4**, **P4-Q4**). Remove any one of the three and the other two stop being
enough.

He will not give his address to someone who has not yet paid (**P3-Q4**). That
single sentence is the reason the prototype unlocks the address on payment
rather than on request.

He has a job. If lending costs him more coordination than it earns him, he stops
— not with a complaint, just silently (**P4-Q1**).

### Goals

| | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Turn idle, expensive equipment into either money or space, without selling it outright | P3-Q1, P4-Q1 |
| Know who has his equipment, verified, not self-declared | P3-Q4, P7-Q4 |
| Have a neutral record of condition so a dispute is settled by evidence | P4-Q4, P3-Q7 |
| Spend minutes, not evenings, on the administration | P4-Q1 |
| Keep his home address private until money has changed hands | P3-Q4 |

### Frustrations

| | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Review scores are trivially gamed and he discounts them entirely | P3-Q4 |
| "It was like that when I got it" is unanswerable without a record | P4-Q4 |
| Coordinating a pickup around a working week | P4-Q1, P2-Q2 |
| Seasonal demand means the calendar has to be right or he double-books himself | P6-Q2 |

### Empathy map

| Quadrant | Content |
|---|---|
| **SAYS** | *"Stars don't mean anything to me. Anybody can get five stars renting a tent twice."* (**P3-Q4**) · *"Honestly? I'd want photos. Mine and theirs, at the start."* (**P4-Q4**) · *"I'm not giving my address to someone I've never met before they've paid."* (**P3-Q4**) · *"The SUP comes out maybe twice… and I'm annoyed every time I have to move it."* (**P3-Q1**) |
| **THINKS** | *If it comes back cracked, whose word wins?* (**P4-Q4**) · *Is this worth an evening of messaging?* (**P4-Q1**) · *I might want it in July, so I'm not selling it.* (**P3-Q1**) |
| **DOES** | Moves the SUP out of the way and doesn't sell it · Checks the value of his kit and knows the number (**P3-Q1**) · Would photograph the item himself before handing it over, unprompted (**P3-Q7**) · Declines requests he can't schedule around work (**P4-Q1**) |
| **FEELS** | **Protective** of equipment he chose and paid for (**P3-Q4**) · **Sceptical** of reputation systems (**P3-Q4**) · **Guilty** about the idle asset (**P3-Q1**) · **Reassured** by a paired before/after record (**OBS-P4-T8**) |
| **PAINS** | No verified identity behind a request · No neutral condition record · Coordination cost around a job · Address exposure before payment · Double-booking risk |
| **GAINS** | Identity + deposit + evidence, present together · A calendar that shows buffer days so turnaround is visible · A return screen that pairs against the handover · A request inbox that already carries the renter's fit and trust tier |

### What he needs from the design

Verification held and displayed (**S-32**, **S-41**), a deposit or protection
choice that puts real money at stake (**S-09**), a timestamped condition record
at handover *and* a paired comparison at return (**S-15**, **S-18**), a master
calendar including buffer days (**S-27**), a request inbox with the renter's fit
and tier already attached (**S-28**), and address release gated on payment
(**S-13**).

## Persona 3 — Mala Thangarajah — *The Custodian*

**Category:** SC-3, Operator / Custodian
**Composite of:** P6, P7

|  |  |
|---|---|
| **Age** | 41 |
| **Location** | Jaffna; works between a beach-front shack and a university store room |
| **Role** | Equipment coordinator — ~60 items on a manual register — and part-time operator of a small rental shack with 11 boards |
| **Tech** | Laptop at a desk, phone on the beach. Lives in a spreadsheet she does not like |
| **Volume** | Turns away four people on a Saturday and rents nothing on a Tuesday |

### Her situation

Mala is the stakeholder most design projects skip, and she is where trust is
actually manufactured. She already runs, on paper, the exact stack Roshan asked
for: a signed form, a student ID number held, and a walk-around before anything
leaves the store (**P7-Q4**).

Her problem is not that the process doesn't exist. It is that the process has no
memory. The argument happens three days later, when the board is already back on
the rack and nobody can produce what it looked like on Friday (**P6-Q10**). If it
is in WhatsApp, it is gone (**P7-Q10**).

She does not want a chat thread. She wants to open a thing, see what was agreed,
and close it (**P7-Q10**). That is a work queue, and it is desk work, not phone
work.

She also reads the app as an ally rather than a competitor: it is a way to fill
the days when nobody walks in (**P6-Q2**). That reframing produced the GearPoint
collection point in the prototype, and it did not come from the brief.

### Goals

| | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Resolve a damage claim in minutes, with evidence, and have the decision recorded | P6-Q10, P7-Q10 |
| Replace a paper register that cannot be searched or audited | P7-Q4, P7-Q10 |
| Fill weekday idle capacity from a demand pool she doesn't have to find herself | P6-Q2 |
| Be able to show *why* a decision was made, to either party, afterwards | P7-Q10 |

### Frustrations

| | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Disputes surface days late, when the evidence window has closed | P6-Q10 |
| Conversation-shaped tools lose state; nothing can be reopened or audited | P7-Q10 |
| Cannot hold stock for someone who may not arrive | P6-Q2 |
| Manual registers don't scale past about sixty items | P7-Q4 |

### Empathy map

| Quadrant | Content |
|---|---|
| **SAYS** | *"If it's in WhatsApp it's gone. I need to be able to open a thing, see what was agreed, and close it."* (**P7-Q10**) · *"The argument always happens three days later, when the board's already back on the rack."* (**P6-Q10**) · *"I can't hold a board for someone who might not turn up."* (**P6-Q2**) |
| **THINKS** | *Whose word am I taking here, and can I show my working?* (**P7-Q10**) · *Saturday I turn away four people; Tuesday I rent nothing.* (**P6-Q2**) · *A helmet that's loose is worse than no helmet.* (**P7-Q3**) |
| **DOES** | Keeps a signed form and an ID number for every issue (**P7-Q4**) · Does a walk-around before the kit leaves the store (**P7-Q4**) · Maintains a spreadsheet of ~60 items · Refuses phone bookings she cannot guarantee (**P6-Q2**) |
| **FEELS** | **Exposed** when a dispute has no record (**P6-Q10**) · **Frustrated** by tools that forget (**P7-Q10**) · **Responsible** for safety in a way renters are not (**P7-Q3**) · **Opportunistic** about weekday capacity (**P6-Q2**) |
| **PAINS** | No audit trail · Evidence arrives late or not at all · Conversation tools lose state · Manual register doesn't scale · Idle weekday capacity |
| **GAINS** | A claims queue with the before/after evidence already attached · A recorded decision with a stated reason, visible to both parties · A desk-shaped interface, not a phone one · Bookings that are commitments |

### What she needs from the design

A queue rather than an inbox (**S-40**), the handover and return evidence
attached to the claim rather than hunted for (**S-15**, **S-18**, **S-18b**,
**S-34**), a recorded decision with a stated reason, and a desktop layout —
which is why **S-40** is the one 1440×1024 screen in a phone-first prototype.
