# GearLoop — 10 Simple Interview Questions

The short version. Plain words, no jargon, nothing to memorise. You can read
these straight off the page to anyone — a friend, a classmate, someone at the
beach — and still get real answers.

The other two guides ([Version A](interview-questions.md),
[Version B](interview-questions-v2.md)) are longer and more thorough. This one is
for when you need to just go and talk to people.

---

## How to ask

Five rules. That's all of it.

1. **Ask "why?" after every single answer.** The first answer is polite. The
   second one is true.
2. **Ask about the past, not the future.** "When did you last…" beats "would
   you…". People are bad at predicting themselves and good at remembering.
3. **Stay quiet.** When they stop talking, count to three before you speak.
   They'll usually keep going, and the second half is the good half.
4. **Don't defend the app.** If they don't like something, write it down and say
   "that's useful, tell me more."
5. **Write their words, not your summary.** "I'd never lend my board to a
   stranger" is worth ten lines of your notes.

**Say this at the start:**

> I'm a student doing a design project — an app for renting and swapping sports
> gear between people. I want honest opinions, not nice ones. About 20 minutes.
> Is it okay if I record?

## The 10 questions

### 1. What sports or outdoor gear do you own that you hardly ever use?
*Is there anything to rent out in the first place?*

### 2. When was the last time you needed equipment you didn't own — what did you do?
*What the app has to beat: borrowing, a rental shop, buying, or giving up.*

### 3. Would you lend your own gear to someone you've never met?
*The whole app depends on the answer. Ask why either way.*

### 4. What would make you feel safe doing that?
*Let them invent the trust features before you show them yours.*

### 5. Have you ever used the wrong size equipment? What happened?
*Is a size warning a safety feature or just a convenience?*

### 6. Would you leave a deposit to rent something? How much is too much?
*Money is where bookings die. Find the number.*

### 7. If gear came back damaged, how should the two of you sort out who pays?
*See if they invent "photos before and after" on their own.*

### 8. Would you swap gear you don't use for something else instead of renting it out?
*Swapping is the riskiest idea in the app. This is its test.*

### 9. What would you pay to rent a surfboard for a weekend — and what would you expect to earn from lending yours?
*Two numbers, one question. Ask how they got there.*

### 10. Here's the app — have a try. What's confusing, and would you use it?
*Hand over the phone and say nothing. Watch where they slow down.*

## After each interview

Straight away, before you forget, write down three things:

1. **The best quote** — their exact words.
2. **The one thing that surprised you.**
3. **Yes / no / maybe** — would they actually use it?

Do that for 6–8 people. Then look for the answer that keeps coming back. That
repeated answer is your finding, and it's what goes in the report.

**One tip for the write-up:** include the people who said *no*. A report where
everyone loved it looks like you only asked your friends. One person explaining
why they'd never use it is worth more than five who said it was nice.
